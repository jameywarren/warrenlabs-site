The problem

A graph is a line. Listening is personal.

Measurement sites can tell you what your headphones do. None can tell you what they dofor you, and none let you actually hear the difference.

01

Numbers aren't a sound

A frequency-response curve is a chart to read, not a thing to hear. Two people looking at the same line want two different tunings, and neither can tell from the graph.

02

One "correct" target

Diffuse-field. Neutral. Somebody's house curve. Every tool hands you one preset curve and calls it right. Your gear and your taste never got a vote.

03

Nowhere to actually listen

Most tools stop at data and presets. The one thing that matters, hearing it on your music and choosing, is left to you.

The best tuning for your headphones isn't the "right" one.
It's the one you'd pick with your eyes closed.
How it works

Four steps to your sound.

The graph becomes the surface you're hearing through, not a chart you're reading. Everything runs in real time on your own music.

The ear exam

Like an eye exam, for tuning.

About a dozen short comparisons (A or B?) on your own music, gain-matched so loudness never tips your hand. No sliders, no theory. You do the deciding.

  • ◆Blind & honest. Every comparison is loudness-matched, so louder never masquerades as better.
  • ◆On your music. The A/B runs on the track that's already playing, the passages you actually know.
  • ◆Seamless switching. Crossfades between tunings instead of muting, so the only thing that changes is the sound.
  • ◆Yours to keep. The result is a personal EQ curve, saved per headphone. With Pro it exports as a standard AutoEq file and travels to your phone, DAP or dongle.

Voice match

Hear another headphone’s tuning on your own cans.

Curious how a different headphone is voiced? We measured both, sowe take the difference between the two and correct for it, loudness-matched. That is voice matching, and it lets you hear how another headphone’s balance would sit on the pair already on your head: blind, on your own music.

  • ◆Match the tonal signature, not a literal clone. You borrow another tuning’s balance (its bass, mids and treble) and hear it on your gear.
  • ◆Honest about the limits. EQ matches tonal balance, not resolution, soundstage, distortion, or driver physics. No $80 can becomes a $4k flagship, and we won’t pretend otherwise.
  • ◆A starting point, not a verdict. Use a reference voicing as your baseline, then keep tuning by ear until it’s yours.

Whose measurements

Six headphones. All of them ours.

Every curve in Attune is our own measurement, taken here on a miniDSP EARS Pro, with the rig’s uncertainty drawn on the graph rather than hidden. Nothing is scraped from a public database. Six we measured ourselves beats five thousand we didn’t.

01

Measured here, on one rig

One fixture, one stated seating rule, both channels kept. The method is written down and you can check it, including the parts that are not flattering.

02

Yours isn’t listed? Import it

CSV, AutoEq or squig. Attune voice-matches to an imported curve exactly as it would to a bundled one. Free, permanently, and it will not become a paid feature.

03

Or send us the pair

We measure your exact unit on our rig and publish the result, flattering or not, then add it. About a week. See how that works.
