Our take

If you want to hear what a recording actually holds, the HD 650 gets out of the way. Bass is even and unboosted, the mids are its whole reason for being, and the treble is polite, some call it dark. EQ can lift that top end without breaking the tuning, which is exactly what Attune is for.

Common questions

Straight answers — the same ones our measurements support.

Does the Sennheiser HD 650 need an amp?

It benefits from one but does not require a big one. At 300 Ω it wants voltage more than current, so a decent USB-C dongle or any competent headphone output drives it to honest levels. A dedicated amp mainly buys headroom, not a different tuning.

HD 650 vs HD 600: what is the difference?

Small and real. The 650 is a touch warmer with slightly more upper-bass and a gentler top end; the 600 is a hair brighter and more forward. On the graph they are close cousins: pick by preference, not price.

Is the HD 650 good for mixing?

For tonal balance and midrange decisions, yes. It is a longtime studio reference. Its relaxed treble means you should check your highs on a second source, which is true of most headphones.

Why does the treble look like it rolls off?

Part is the headphone’s relaxed voicing, part is the measurement rig: above ~10 kHz, fixture and seating variance dominate, so treat the top octave as directional, not exact.
