Our take

The PM-3 measures like something built to a brief rather than to a trend: bass is level rather than boosted, the lower mids sit a little back, and there is a clear presence lift around 3 kHz that keeps voices forward. Above 8 kHz it eases off, so it reads as smooth rather than airy, which closed planars usually do. It is a headphone that responds well to a small amount of correction rather than a rescue, which is the easiest kind to tune.

The headphone is seated deliberately for the best fit, the way someone who cares about sound adjusts a headphone before they settle in to listen, rather than placed at random, and the published curve is a single real seating chosen by an objective rule, never a blend. Both channels are measured and stored separately; the line drawn here is their average. Raw response, normalized to 0 dB at 1 kHz. An EARS Pro is not an industry reference coupler such as a GRAS 45CA, so read this as a house reference: repeatable against our other EARS Pro measurements, not directly comparable to another lab. Seating repeatability was characterized in August 2026 and turned out to be a property of the headphone rather than of the rig: reseating moves the midrange by about 0.06 dB on some models and 0.32 dB on others, so no single variance band is drawn here. Common questions

Straight answers, the same ones our measurements support.

Does the OPPO PM-3 need an amp?

No. At 26 Ω and around 102 dB/mW it was designed to run off a phone, and it does. An amp buys headroom, not a different tuning.

Is it still worth buying?

OPPO left the audio business, so it is a used-market proposition now. The measurement is here so you can decide on what it does rather than on nostalgia.

Why does the top octave look rolled off?

Partly the headphone, partly the rig. Above ~8 kHz fixture and seating variance dominate any measurement, so read the top octave as a direction, not a number.
