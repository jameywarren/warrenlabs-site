Privacy

Chain, by Warren Labs. Last updated 11 September 2026.

Chain keeps a record of audio gear you own: what it is, what you paid, what you changed, what it sounds like to you, and photographs of it. Some of that is private to you. Some of it is published, by you, on purpose. This page says which is which, because that is the only part of a privacy policy that matters.

The short version

  • Chain has no account and asks for no email address or password. You can open it, record your whole collection, and never tell us anything.
  • Your collection stays on your device unless you choose to publish something.
  • Nothing is sold, ever, to anybody. There is no advertising in Chain, no analytics, and no third party code that reports on you. We have not installed a single tracking library and do not intend to.
  • We cannot read your collection. That is not a promise about our conduct. It is a property of where the data sits.

What stays on your device

Everything you record about gear you own:

  • Your chains, and the units in them
  • Serial numbers, marks, what you paid, when you bought it, who you bought it from
  • Condition grades, your notes, what is in the box, parts you have fitted
  • Photographs you take of your own gear
  • Measurements you take, and the rig you took them on
  • Which posts you have kept, and who you have blocked

This is held in a file inside the app on your device. If you delete Chain, it goes with it.

What is published, and only when you publish it

Three things leave your device, and each one is an act you take deliberately:

A listing. When you list something for sale, the listing is readable by everybody using Chain. It carries the item, the price, the grade you claimed, your condition notes, what is included, and the photographs you took for it. It carries a shortened form of the serial rather than the whole number.

A post. When you post a photograph to the wire, the photograph, what you wrote, the gear you tagged in it and the place on your profile are readable by everybody using Chain.

A message. When you message somebody about a listing, that conversation is stored so both of you can read it.

Your profile goes with all three: the handle you chose, the place you typed, your one line, and your picture if you took one.

What we can see

We cannot see your collection. Anything private is held on your device and, if you are signed in to iCloud, in your own private iCloud container. Apple's design means the operator of an app cannot read a user's private container. There is no key we are choosing not to use.

We can see what you publish, in the same way any other person using Chain can: listings, posts, and your profile. We can also read a message if it is reported to us, which is the only circumstance in which we would.

We can see reports you send us, including what you said and which listing, post or person it was about. Somebody who has been reported is not told who reported them.

The camera, and your photo library

Chain asks for the camera and uses it for exactly one thing: photographing gear.

Chain has no access to your photo library and does not ask for any. There is no library picker anywhere in the app, including for your profile picture. Every photograph in Chain was taken inside Chain, which is the point: a photograph taken here is evidence about an object, and one chosen from a library is not.

Photographs are checked on your device before they are kept. Chain uses Apple's Sensitive Content Analysis, which runs entirely on your device and sends nothing anywhere. The result never leaves your phone and we never see it. This check only runs if you have Sensitive Content Warning switched on in your own iOS settings; where you have not, no check happens and Chain does not pretend otherwise.

Location

Chain has no access to your location and never asks for it. There is no location code in the app.

The place shown on your profile and your listings is a town you typed yourself. You can change it or remove it whenever you like, in Settings.

What Chain does not do

  • No advertising, and no advertising identifiers
  • No analytics, no crash reporting to us, no usage tracking
  • No third party SDKs of any kind
  • No selling, renting or sharing of anything, to anybody, for any purpose
  • No profile building, no inferring things about you, no recommendations based on what you looked at
  • No like counts, follower counts or scores of any kind. This is a design decision rather than a privacy one, but the effect is the same: Chain does not keep a tally about you

Children

Chain is rated 13 and over and is not designed for or directed at children.

Changing or deleting your data

Anything private: delete the app. Everything it kept on your device goes with it.

Anything you published: email us and we will remove it. Listings, posts, messages and your profile.

We would rather offer a button than an email address, and there will be one. Until there is, this page describes what actually exists rather than what is planned.

Requests are answered by a person, usually within a few days:jamey@warrenlabs.com

Other people's data

If you photograph somebody else's gear, or write about them, the ordinary rules apply: do not post what is not yours to post. Every listing, post, message and profile in Chain carries a report control and a block control. Blocking somebody removes their listings and posts from what you see immediately, and it is enforced where the data is stored rather than merely hidden on screen.
