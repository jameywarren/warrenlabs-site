How the measurements are made

A miniDSP EARS Pro with per-unit serial-matched calibration, using IEC 60318-4 couplers, the coupler standard the industry rigs use.

  • Seated for best fit, deliberately. The headphone is adjusted for seal and sound the way someone settling in to listen would adjust it, not placed at random.
  • One real seating, never a blend. The published curve is the medoid of the run, between 10 and 24 reseats depending on the headphone: an actual achievable placement the data elected, not a computed middle. Averaging reseats publishes a bass shelf nobody will ever hear, and on one headphone here it published a treble peak no seating ever produced.
  • Raw and full band. Not compensated to any target, normalized to 0 dB at 1 kHz. Both channels are measured and stored separately.
  • Curves from two rigs never share an axis. The offset between this bench and the old HeadRoom fixture has not been measured, so a cross-rig difference cannot be quoted and is not drawn.
  • Repeatability is measured, and it is a property of the headphone. The instrument contributes 0.002 dB. Reseating moves the midrange by 0.06 dB on an HD 800 and 0.32 dB on a PM-3, so there is no single variance band to draw over every curve and we do not draw one. An EARS Pro is a house reference, not an industry reference coupler.
  • Two of the six are older and say so. Four rows are regenerated from verified, archived runs. The SR60x and RS1x predate that method and have no archived run behind them. They are labelled, not quietly mixed in.

The published set is small and says so. Every curve carries the rig that produced it, the operator, and its stated limits.
