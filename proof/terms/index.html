What Proof's numbers mean, and what they are not for

This section is the boundary of what Proof claims. It is not a retraction of it.

What Proof claims, and we stand behind it. Every reading Proof prints is reported against a noise-and-distortion floor that the instrument measured on the equipment in front of it. A reading that is clear of that floor is printed with the number of digits the floor entitles it to. A reading at or under the floor is printed as an inequality with no digits, because the honest statement is "quieter than this instrument can hear". Most analysers print a number there. Proof does not, and that refusal is the product. Nothing on this page is an admission that Proof's numbers are vague, unreliable or not to be believed. They are stated with their uncertainty attached, which is more than most tools in this class do.

What that does not make Proof. It is not an accredited, certified or traceable instrument. We provide no calibration certificate and no traceability to a national standard, and we hold no accreditation of any kind. Proof measures through hardware we did not make, in a room we have not seen, operated by someone we cannot supervise. Where the app supports calibration (sound pressure level can be calibrated against an acoustic calibrator you supply), the accuracy of that calibration is the accuracy of your calibrator and your microphone, not something we can warrant.

So, plainly: do not use Proof as the instrument of record where the number itself carries legal, regulatory or safety weight. That includes, and is not limited to:

  • workplace or environmental noise-exposure compliance, and any measurement a regulator will act on;
  • product safety certification, type approval, or conformity assessment;
  • building-code or environmental-health acoustic compliance;
  • medical, audiological or hearing-health decisions of any kind;
  • evidence in a dispute where the measurement itself is the thing being argued about.

Those uses need an instrument with documented calibration and traceability, usually to a defined class or type, operated to a published method. Proof is not one and does not claim to be. The sound pressure level meter and the noise dosimeter report a measured quantity and prescribe nothing; they are not a substitute for a class- or type-rated sound level meter where one is required.

About the records specifically. A Proof record carries a content hash. That hash proves the document has not been altered since it was written, and anyone can check it without our software. It does not prove the equipment was set up correctly, that the right thing was measured, or that the person holding it is who they say they are, and the format makes no such claim. A record is a statement about what this instrument measured, on this equipment, at that moment. Treat it as exactly that.

The distinction we are drawing is between honesty and authority. Proof is honest about its own uncertainty. It is not an authority, and no amount of honesty makes it one.
