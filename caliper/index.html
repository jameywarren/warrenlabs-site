Caliper

A measurement you can believe

Caliper measures a headphone on an acoustic fixture and tells you how much of what you are looking at is real. Free, for Mac and iPhone.

Download Caliper on the App Store — free, one purchase covers Mac and iPhone.

The problem it solves

Any app can draw a curve. The hard part is knowing whether the same headphone, measured again, would give you the same curve — because if it would not, the differences you are reading belong to how it was sitting on the fixture, not to the headphone.

Most published headphone measurements are a single curve with no statement of uncertainty. Caliper asks for a few captures instead of one and spends them working out its own error budget, so every verdict it gives you is measured against a floor it actually established.

How it works

The duplicate pair. Two sweeps of one ear with nothing touched in between. Any difference between them is the instrument and the room, not the headphone — which makes it the smallest difference the run is entitled to call real. A run will not complete without one.

Seatings. Take the headphone off, put it back on, measure again. The spread between seatings is the honest uncertainty of the measurement, and it is usually larger than people expect. How many you need is derived from how much yours actually disagree, not fixed by a rule — an easy headphone may need one, a difficult one eight.

A tier, computed and never asserted. What a run earns depends on what it holds. The app says what is missing and what to do next, and a capture taken in any order counts for whatever it satisfies.

What it will not do

  • It will not smooth a measurement unless you ask. Smoothing is a display choice and the stored data stays raw.
  • It will not let you measure half-calibrated. One capsule corrected and the other not makes every left-versus-right difference an artefact of the calibration rather than the headphone, so it refuses.
  • It will not judge distortion it cannot see. At low drive the harmonics sit in the noise, so it says the signal-to-noise is too low to tell rather than blaming the driver.
  • It will not publish synthesised data. There is a simulated fixture for trying the app without hardware; every capture it makes is stamped, and exporting a run that holds one is refused.

Price

Free. No paid tier, no subscription, no in-app purchase, on both Mac and iPhone.

Privacy

No account, no analytics, no telemetry, and nothing uploaded in the background. The one thing Caliper ever sends is a measurement you choose to submit, and it shows you what is being sent first. The privacy policy is specific about it.
