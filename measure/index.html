The list

What we’re asking for.

We are building this corpus from scratch, one headphone at a time. These are the models we most want on the bench first, and the reason each one earns a slot. Send one of these and the measurement is free, with shipping covered both ways. Anything else you own is $89.

Modern reference / most-compared open backs

  • Sennheiser HD 800 SP1The single most-compared headphone above $1,000 and the default soundstage/imaging reference in every review that mentions staging.
  • Sennheiser HD 560SP1Probably the most-recommended sub-$200 open back of the last six years, and widely used as an informal near-neutral reference people EQ from.
  • Sennheiser HD 490 ProP1Sennheiser's current pro open back, ships with two pad sets and a stated target, and is the model most often benchmarked in mixing/mastering roundups now.
  • Sennheiser HD 660S2P2The live 600-series flagship and the permanent subject of is it worth it over the HD 6XX.
  • Audio-Technica ATH-R70xaP2The R70x sat unusually high in Crinacle's list (A- tier, $350) for a cheap open back; the a revision is Audio-Technica's current professional reference and has noticeably thinner independent measurement coverage than the model it replaced.
  • Focal Utopia (2022) + Focal Clear MGP2The dynamic-driver counterweight to the planar-dominated top end.
  • Beyerdynamic DT 1990 Pro MKIIP2Beyerdynamic's current open studio flagship on the new 30-ohm Tesla.45 driver, a genuine break from the archive's 250/600-ohm DT 880/DT 990 lineage, and the model that inherits their studio slot.

Current budget and midrange workhorses (what people actually buy)

  • Drop Sennheiser HD 6XXP1Almost certainly the most-owned audiophile headphone in existence, and it recirculates at $130–170 constantly.
  • HiFiMan HE400seP1The ~$110 planar that is the default answer to what is my first planar.
  • Philips SHP9500P2The perennial sub-$100 open back recommendation, with a second life as a gaming/modding platform.
  • Koss KPH40 Utility (and KPH30i)P2The modern cult budget king that has largely displaced the Porta Pro in recommendations.
  • AKG K612 ProP2The surviving cheap AKG open back and the practical successor to the archive's K701/K702.

Planars

  • HiFiMan SundaraP1The reference point for affordable planars, full stop.
  • HiFiMan Edition XSP1Probably the best-selling audiophile planar of the decade at its $250–400 street price, and the most common Sundara or XS question in the hobby.
  • HiFiMan Arya OrganicP1Routinely described as the best-measuring headphone per dollar at street price, and the anchor of the $800–1,600 tier.
  • Audeze LCD-X (2021 revision)P1The archive's LCD-X is the original; the 2021 revision is a different tuning and is the unit actually in working studios.
  • Moondrop ParaP2The Chi-Fi planar that undercut HiFiMan on tuning at ~$340.
  • Meze Empyrean II / Meze PoetP3Meze has zero presence in the archive despite being one of the three high-end planar houses people actually shortlist.

Closed backs and studio staples of the current era

  • AKG K371P1The most-cited measures right and costs $150 headphone in existence, tuned to the Harman over-ear target and used as the closed-back benchmark in most modern comparisons.
  • Beyerdynamic DT 770 Pro, current 80Ω and 250ΩP1The archive has the 600Ω variant, which is discontinued.
  • Sennheiser HD 620SP2Sennheiser's new closed 600-series entry at ~$350, positioned directly against the DT 770 / M50x tier and generating a lot of closed HD 650?
  • Sony MDR-M1P2Sony's actual modern successor to the MDR-7506 that the archive holds.
  • Dan Clark Audio E3 (or Stealth)P2DCA is absent from the archive entirely and is one of the two or three brands that define the modern closed high end.
  • Sony WH-1000XM6 and Apple AirPods Max 2P2The two products that most non-enthusiasts mean when they say headphones.

Genuinely contested or measurement-scarce

  • Grado SR325x / Hemp / RS1xP1The archive has five Grados, all pre-x-series.
  • HiFiMan Susvara (and Susvara Unveiled)P2The top-of-market reference, and one of the few headphones where 5128-vs-711 results are documented as behaving oddly relative to other models, including unusual low-frequency variance under imperfect seal.
  • ZMF Caldera / Atrium, with the full pad setP2ZMF has no third-party presence in the archive and comparatively little anywhere: the response is strongly pad-dependent, wood cups introduce unit variation, and the manufacturer publishes its own measurements.
  • Stax SR-L700 MkII or SR-X9000 (plus energizer)P3Electrostatics need a dedicated drive setup, so third-party data is thin and rarely comparable.
  • RAAL 1995 Immanis / MagnaP3Ribbon drivers, very high hype-to-data ratio, and an unusual transformer-fed drive requirement.
  • Shokz OpenRun Pro 2 (open-ear / bone conduction)P3A fast-growing mass-market category with essentially no rigorous published measurement, partly because standard couplers do not apply cleanly.

P1 is what we want first. P2 is the second wave. P3 fills a real gap when one turns up. Not on the list and still want yours measured? That is the $89 lane, and the answer is yes.

Questions

The obvious ones.

Does it cost me anything?

It depends which lane you are in. If the model is on the requested list we cover shipping both ways, inbound and back. If you are sending a unit we have not asked for, the $89 covers the measurement and you cover shipping.

Is my headphone insured in transit?

Yes, both directions. You tell us the value, we insure to it, and we photograph the unit on arrival and again before it ships back. Documented, not a gotcha.

How long will you keep it?

We measure and ship it back within about five business days of arrival. If anything will take longer, we tell you before you send it.

What if the measurement is unflattering?

We publish it anyway. Honest graphs are the whole point. We show exactly how a headphone measures, good or bad. What we will not do is dress it up.

What if you can’t publish mine?

Sometimes a unit arrives damaged, won’t seal on the rig, or gives data we don’t trust. If that happens we tell you why, you still get your unit’s curve, and we simply don’t publish it.

Do I need an account?

No. There is no account, no profile and no sign-up. Tell us on the intake form how you want to be credited, and that is all we need.

What headphones can you measure?

Over-ear and on-ear headphones. Not IEMs. An in-ear’s response is decided by how deep it sits and how well the tip seals, and our fixture is an artificial ear with no way to control either. Measuring them here would produce a confident-looking number we could not stand behind, so we don’t. If you’re not sure yours qualifies, tell us in the form and we’ll say.

Do you have old measurements from HeadRoom?

We hold them: 319 headphones measured on a Head Acoustics HMS II.3 around 2010–2013, but we no longer publish them as measurements, and we don’t mix them with anything measured here. They are one unit per model, on a head that no longer exists, with no record of how the headphone was seated. We keep them as a research reference and measure from scratch instead. Your headphone gets measured on our own bench and labeled as such.
